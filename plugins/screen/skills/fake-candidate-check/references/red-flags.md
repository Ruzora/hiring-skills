# Signal table for the red-flag log

Weight is about identity and money consistency only. One Strong signal is enough to pause and verify. It is never enough to decide.

## Strong (from FBI and allied-government guidance on North Korean IT worker schemes)

| Signal | Verification step that resolves it either way |
|---|---|
| Asks to ship the work laptop to an address other than the one verified | Live video call: confirm the reason, see the candidate with ID, ship only to the verified address or have them collect it in person |
| Asks to be paid to a third party's account, a different name, or in crypto | Explain the policy (own-name account or own company only); ask for an account that matches; no exceptions in the first 90 days |
| Bank account or address change shortly after offer or during onboarding | Pause the change; live video re-verification with ID before it takes effect |
| Refuses any video, every time, with no accommodation request | Offer the alternatives (in-person, verified video with a second interviewer, notary or ID service). Continued refusal of all of them goes to the human decision |
| Face on video does not match the ID, or a different person appears in a later round | Second live call with two interviewers and the ID held to the camera; compare with notes from earlier rounds |
| Same phone number or email as another applicant with a different name | Ask both candidates, neutrally, to confirm their contact details; call each number yourself |
| Manipulated video (face edges blur on movement, lips out of sync, filter will not turn off) | Use the deepfake interview liveness steps on a fresh call; see whether it repeats |
| Reference cannot be reached through the employer's own public contact details | Reach the company switchboard or HR; ask for a second referee from the same employer |
| Logins from unexpected countries, many IP addresses, or around the clock after hire | Reduce access first, then a live video check-in; ask the IT owner to review remote-access software on the machine |

## Moderate (consistency signals; verify, do not weigh heavily alone)

| Signal | Verification step |
|---|---|
| CV and LinkedIn disagree on employers, titles or dates | Ask the candidate to walk through the timeline; small date slips are common, different employers are not |
| Rate well below market for the seniority claimed | Ask how they arrived at the rate; check with your range; note only |
| Cannot extend or explain something they wrote or said earlier (the content, not the polish) | Ask a follow-up on their own earlier answer, live; a real person can add detail |
| Behavior or knowledge changes noticeably between calls at different times of day | Schedule the next call at a different time with someone who met them before |
| Very new profiles everywhere, few connections, no one who can confirm the work | Ask for one colleague or manager you can reach through the employer |
| Unusual urgency to skip steps ("can we skip the video, I'll start tomorrow") | Keep the standard steps; a real candidate accepts a 15-minute call |

## Not a signal (remove from the log, say why in one line)

- Accent, non-native English, grammar, or translated-sounding writing on its own. Many excellent engineers write and speak English as a second language. The 2026 alert mentions machine-translation-like profile text, but on its own it cannot tell a fraudster from an honest non-native writer, so it carries no weight unless tied to an identity mismatch above.
- Typed messages more polished than spoken answers. Many people write better than they speak in a second language, and many use grammar tools.
- Country of birth, nationality, ethnicity, name, or a degree from outside the US.
- Living outside the US for a role that allows it.
- A virtual background or blurred background before you have asked everyone to turn it off. Many real candidates use one.
- Nervousness, pauses, or a slow connection by itself.
- No GitHub activity or a thin LinkedIn by itself. Most professional work is private.
- Employment gaps.

Using any item in this list as evidence can amount to national-origin or other discrimination. If the user raises one, acknowledge the worry and point to the identity checks that would answer it fairly.
