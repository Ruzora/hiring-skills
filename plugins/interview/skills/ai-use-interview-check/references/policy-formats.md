# Matching interview formats to an AI stance

## The three stances

| Stance | Means | Enforceable in |
|---|---|---|
| Allowed | Use any AI tool, no need to say how | Any format |
| Allowed with disclosure | Use AI tools; write or say what you used them for and what you changed | Any format; the disclosure is checked in the follow-up conversation |
| Not allowed | No AI assistance during this stage | Live conversation, live exercise with camera on, in person. Not async. |

## Stage by stage

| Typical stage | Recommended stance | Why / what to change |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter or founder screen | Not allowed (conversational) | It's a conversation about the candidate's own history; hidden help shows up as answers that don't survive "tell me more". |
| Async take-home | Allowed with disclosure | A ban is unenforceable. Ask for a short note: what the AI produced, what they changed, what they rejected. Grade the follow-up call. |
| Live coding on a puzzle | Change the format | Puzzle questions are what assistants answer best. Replace with a live change to the candidate's own take-home, or a seeded-bug code review. |
| Code review exercise | Either; say which | Reading code and ranking problems is judgment. If AI is allowed, ask them to show what the tool flagged and which flags they discarded. |
| System design | Not allowed (conversational) | Designs are about trade-offs for your product; probe with changed constraints. |
| Past-work walkthrough | Not allowed (conversational) | The strongest fundamentals check: nobody else knows the candidate's own past project. |
| AI-fluency pairing | Allowed, screen shared, narrated | The point is to watch the steering. |
| In-person round | Not allowed or allowed, by design | Some large companies have added in-person rounds; Google's CEO said in 2025 they would introduce at least one in-person round to check fundamentals (as reported by several outlets). For a small remote team, a live camera-on conversation on the candidate's own work gets most of the benefit at no travel cost. |

## Accommodation

Screen readers, dictation, captioning and similar assistive tools are not "AI use" under any of these stances. The candidate note should invite accommodation requests in advance. For job demonstrations, the EEOC says the employer must provide a reasonable accommodation that does not create undue hardship, or let the applicant describe how they would do the task (https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-preemployment-disability-related-questions-and-medical).
