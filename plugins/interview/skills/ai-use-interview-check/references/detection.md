# Signals worth probing (and what is not evidence)

Rule: a signal is a reason to ask a follow-up, never a conclusion. Probe the same way you would for any candidate, write down what was said, and let the hiring manager decide with the full record.

## Content signals (usable)

| Signal | Probe | Genuine answer looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Polished, complete answer that addresses a slightly different question than the one asked | Re-ask the exact question in different words | Adjusts to the actual question, often with a shorter, less polished answer |
| Every answer has the same structure (overview, three points, summary) regardless of the question | "Give me just the one-sentence version." / "What's the part you'd skip?" | Compresses easily and has a view on what matters. Note: this structure is also common in people coached for interviews or trained in consulting; probe, never score the structure itself |
| Strong first answer, collapse at the second follow-up | "What exactly did you do?" then "What went wrong?" | Gets more specific with each probe: names, numbers, a mistake |
| Past-project story with no friction: no bug, no trade-off, no person | "What would a teammate from that project say you got wrong?" | A real, specific failure or disagreement |
| Cannot modify their own submitted code live | "Change it so that [small new requirement]." | Knows where to make the change within a minute or two, even if the change takes longer |
| Disclosure note says "used AI for boilerplate" but cannot explain the core logic | "Walk me through this function line by line." | Explains intent and edge cases on top of what each line does |
| Answer contains confident specifics that are wrong for the stack or version | "Where did you see that behavior?" | Corrects themselves or explains the context they were thinking of |

## Not evidence. Never record, never score.

- Accent, native language, grammar, word choice
- Eye movement, looking away, not looking at the camera (common with second monitors, notes, neurodivergence and cultural norms)
- Pauses, slow typing, nervousness
- Appearance, background, lighting, camera quality (but see Identity concerns below for face mismatch, lip-sync mismatch and camera refusal)
- Any characteristic tied to disability, national origin, age or other protected traits

If the user's notes include any of these, say they must be removed from the evidence.

## Identity concerns are a different problem

If the concern is that the person on camera is not the person who applied (a different face from an earlier call, refusing camera, face filters), that is identity fraud, not AI use. These signals are not evidence of AI use, but they are real identity red flags: the FBI names lip movement out of sync with audio as a deepfake tell (IC3 PSA, June 2022), and the July 2026 multinational alert on North Korean IT workers names refusing video and photo-ID mismatch. Record them as identity concerns, never delete them, and route them to the same identity step for every finalist. Nothing is ever inferred from accent, appearance or origin. The FBI has warned about deepfakes and stolen identities used to apply for remote IT jobs (IC3 PSA, June 2022) and about North Korean IT workers using AI and face-swapping in video interviews (IC3 PSA, January 2025). Handle it with an identity-verification step applied to every finalist, not with the AI policy.

## Script when you suspect hidden help in a no-AI stage

1. Do not accuse. Say: "Let's switch gears. I'd like to go deeper on something you mentioned."
2. Move to the candidate's own past work or own submission and ask the follow-up ladder: what exactly you did, what went wrong, what you'd change.
3. If needed, introduce a live constraint change: "Suppose [new requirement]. What changes?"
4. Write down what was asked and answered, word for word where possible, and the time. No adjectives about the person.
5. After the call, the hiring manager reviews the notes with the other evidence. Options: proceed, run one more live conversational stage offered on the same terms you'd offer anyone, or end the process with a short, neutral note. Never label the candidate a cheater in writing without an admission or clear proof.
