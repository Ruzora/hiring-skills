# Legal check for interview questions (US, as of 2026)

General information, not legal advice. Federal rules below; many states and cities add more (for example marital status, arrest records, salary history). If the user hires outside the US, say local rules apply and differ.

## Never ask (federal protected characteristics and their proxies)

Under Title VII, the ADEA, the ADA and GINA (EEOC: https://www.eeoc.gov/prohibited-employment-policiespractices) and the INA's anti-discrimination provision, do not ask about, infer or score on:
- Race, color, ethnicity, national origin, ancestry, "where are you from", accent, native language (unless a language is truly required for the job, then test the skill, not the origin)
- Citizenship (beyond the lawful work-authorization questions below)
- Religion, religious holidays
- Sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, pregnancy, plans for children, childcare, marital status, spouse's job
- Age, birth year, graduation year, "how many years until retirement", "digital native"
- Disability, health, medications, past sick leave, workers' comp. The EEOC says an employer may not ask disability-related questions before a conditional job offer (https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-preemployment-disability-related-questions-and-medical).
- Genetic information, family medical history
- Arrest record or criminal history in the interview (timing is controlled by state and city fair-chance laws)
- Salary history (banned in many states and cities; ask expectations instead)

## Ask instead

| Don't ask | Ask instead |
|---|---|
| "Where are you from?" / "Are you a citizen?" | "Are you legally authorized to work in the US?" and "Will you now or in the future require visa sponsorship?" |
| "Do you have kids? Can you handle on-call with a family?" | "This role has on-call one week in four, including some nights. Can you meet that schedule?" (ask every candidate) |
| "How old are you?" / "When did you graduate?" | Nothing. If there is a legal minimum age, "Are you at least 18?" |
| "Any health issues we should know about?" | "Can you perform the essential functions of this job, described here, with or without reasonable accommodation?" |
| "What do you make now?" | "What salary range are you looking for?" |
| "Is English your first language?" | "This role writes customer-facing docs in English. Tell me about documentation you've written." |
| "Do you observe any holidays?" | "The team works [hours/days]. Can you meet that schedule?" |
| "Have you ever been arrested?" | Nothing in the interview. Follow your state's fair-chance rules for any background check later. |
| "Would you fit in with our young team?" | "Describe the team setup where you did your best work." |

## How to flag

For each problem question return: the question, the problem in one line (which category), and the rewrite. If a question is fine, do not comment on it. If the user's notes about a candidate mention a protected trait (accent, age, family, health, origin), say it must not be part of the evaluation and remove it from the evidence.
