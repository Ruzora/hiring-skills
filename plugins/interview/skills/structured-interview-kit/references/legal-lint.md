# Legal lint for interview questions (US, as of 2026)

General information, not legal advice. Federal law below; many states and cities add protected categories (for example marital status, arrest records) and ban salary-history questions. Outside the US, flag that local rules apply.

## Flag any question that asks about, or lets the interviewer infer

| Category | Law | Typical bad question | Lawful alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| National origin, citizenship, ancestry, accent | Title VII; INA anti-discrimination provision | "Where are you from originally?" "Is that accent...?" "Are you a citizen?" | "Are you legally authorized to work in the US?" and "Will you now or in the future require sponsorship?" |
| Age (40+) | ADEA | "What year did you graduate?" "How long until you'd retire?" "Are you a digital native?" | Ask about the skills directly. |
| Sex, pregnancy, family status | Title VII (EEOC: questions on marital status or children may violate Title VII if used to limit opportunities) | "Do you have kids?" "Any plans to start a family?" "Will your partner relocate?" | "This role has on-call one week in four including nights. Can you meet that schedule?" Ask every candidate. |
| Disability, health | ADA (no disability-related questions before a conditional offer) | "Any health conditions?" "How many sick days did you take?" | "Can you perform the essential functions of this role, with or without reasonable accommodation?" |
| Religion | Title VII | "Which holidays do you observe?" | "The team works Monday-Friday, 9-5 Eastern. Can you meet that schedule?" |
| Genetic information | GINA | "Does anything run in your family?" | Do not ask. |
| Arrest or criminal record | State and city fair-chance laws | "Have you ever been arrested?" | Not in interviews. Follow local rules for any later background check. |
| Salary history | Banned in many states and cities | "What are you paid now?" | "What range are you looking for?" |
| "Culture fit" | Not illegal by itself, but a common route to proxy bias | "Would you fit our young, scrappy team?" | Name the behavior: "Tell me about a time you shipped with very little direction." |

Sources: EEOC prohibited practices https://www.eeoc.gov/prohibited-employment-policiespractices ; EEOC marital status and children https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/practices/inquiries_marital_status.cfm ; EEOC pre-offer disability questions https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-preemployment-disability-related-questions-and-medical

## Also lint the interviewer notes

If notes or scoring comments mention a protected trait or a proxy (accent, age, looks, family, health, religion, origin, "overqualified" as code for age), flag it and remove it from the evidence. It must not affect the score.

## Accommodation

If a candidate asks for an accommodation (extra time, a different format, captioning, assistive tools), the employer has to consider it. For a job demonstration, the EEOC says the employer must provide a reasonable accommodation that does not create undue hardship, or let the applicant describe how they would perform the function. Add one line to the candidate email inviting accommodation requests.
