# Reference question bank

## Core questions (ask every referee, in this order)

1. **Relationship:** "How did you work with them, for how long, and what was your role relative to theirs?" (Check against the CV.)
2. **Their actual scope:** "What were they responsible for, and what's the most important thing they built or fixed while you worked together?" Follow-up: "What part of that was theirs specifically?"
3. **Calibration:** "Thinking of engineers at their level you've worked with, would you put them in the top 5%, 10%, 25% or 50%?" Follow-up if they hedge: "What would have put them a band higher?"
4. **Coaching:** "What did you coach them on, or what feedback did they get more than once?" If "nothing": "If you were their manager again, what would you work on with them first?"
5. **Rehire:** "If you were building a team tomorrow, would you hire them again? For what kind of role?" Write down the exact words and whether there was a pause.

## Targeted questions by risk

**Scope of ownership / "did they really lead X"**
- "Who led the <project>? Who made the main design decisions?"
- "What happened when it broke in production? Who was on point?"
- Vague answer follow-up: "If I asked them to walk me through <decision>, would they know why it was done that way?"

**Quality under pressure**
- "Tell me about a deadline where something had to give. What did they cut, and what did they refuse to cut?"
- "How often did their code come back from review or production with problems?"

**Ambiguity / need for direction**
- "When they got a loosely defined problem, what did they do in the first week?"
- "How much direction did they need compared with others at their level?"

**Collaboration and disagreement**
- "Tell me about a time they disagreed with you or the team. How did it end?"
- "How did people on other teams (product, support) describe working with them?"

**Leadership / mentoring (for a report or peer)**
- "What did you learn from them?" (for a former report)
- "How did they make the people around them better? Example?"

**Learning speed (mid-level)**
- "What was the newest thing they had to learn on the job, and how long until they were productive with it?"

**Reliability (keep it about work output, never about absences or health)**
- "When they committed to a date, how often did it hold? What did they do when it slipped?"

**AI-assisted work (if relevant)**
- "How did they use AI coding tools, and how did they check what the tool produced?"

## Questions that sound fine but get no signal (skip them)
- "Is she a team player?" (everyone says yes)
- "What are her strengths?" (gets the CV back)
- "Would you recommend her?" (they agreed to be a reference; yes is the default)

## Never ask (and what to do instead)

| Don't ask | Why | Ask instead, if job-related |
|---|---|---|
| How many sick days / absences / leave | Disability, medical and family leave protections | "When they committed to a date, how often did it hold?" |
| Health, stress, mental health, "any personal issues" | Disability | Nothing |
| Family, kids, pregnancy, marital status | Sex / pregnancy | Nothing |
| Age, "how old", "near retirement" | Age 40+ | Nothing |
| Where they are from, citizenship, native language | National origin / citizenship status | Work authorization is confirmed with the candidate only, on Form I-9 after the offer |
| Religion, holidays observed | Religion | Nothing |
| Arrests, criminal history | Fair-chance laws control timing and method | Only through a lawful, consented background check |
| Salary, bonus, equity at that job | Salary-history bans cover asking through others in many places | Nothing; ask the candidate for expectations |
| Workers' comp claims, union activity, complaints filed | Retaliation and labor-law protections | Nothing |

**If the referee volunteers any of the above:** "Thanks, I'm going to keep this to their work, so I won't note that. Can you tell me more about how they handled <project>?" Do not write it down. Do not let it influence the decision.

## Reading answers

| You hear | It usually means | Do |
|---|---|---|
| "Absolutely, I'd hire them tomorrow, for any role on my team" | Strong signal | Record verbatim |
| "Yes, in the right environment" / "for the right role" | Reservation | Ask: "What's the wrong environment?" |
| Pause, then "sure" | Reservation | Ask the ranking question again with a band |
| Praise only for effort, attitude or reliability (for a senior) | Possible skill gap | Ask about the hardest technical problem they solved |
| Can't name a weakness or disagreement after two tries | Unhelpful or not a close colleague | Weight lower; recheck the relationship |
| Contradicts title, dates, or role on a project | Factual mismatch | Pause the offer; ask the candidate neutrally |
| "Company policy: dates and title only" | Common at large companies | Confirm dates and title; ask for a former colleague who has since left |
