# Equity explainer and negotiation bank

## Equity in plain words (template to fill with the user's numbers)

Use the candidate's actual numbers. Where one is missing, keep the formula and write [ask us] rather than guessing.

> **Your grant:** <N> stock options, which is about <P>% of the company today (<N> divided by <total fully diluted shares> fully diluted shares, meaning every share and option that exists or is promised).
>
> **Vesting:** you earn the options over <4> years. Nothing vests for the first <12> months (the "cliff"); on your <1-year> anniversary <25%> vests at once, then the rest vests monthly. If you leave before the cliff, you leave with none.
>
> **Strike price:** options are the right to buy shares at a fixed price, set by the board at grant based on an independent valuation (a "409A"). If the strike is <$S> and you vest <V> options, buying them costs <V x S>. You only gain if the shares are later worth more than that.
>
> **If you leave:** you usually have <90 days, per our plan> to buy vested options, or they expire. [Check the plan; some companies give longer.]
>
> **Dilution:** when we raise money, new shares are created, so your percentage goes down. The number of options stays the same. A rough rule: each priced round dilutes everyone, often by the size of the stake sold to new investors.
>
> **Why the percentage today is not a dollar value:** investors usually hold preferred shares that are paid back first in a sale. Common shares (what options become) get what is left. An example, only to show the math: if the company sold for <$X> and nothing were owed to preferred holders, <P>% would be <$X x P%> before taxes and the cost to exercise. Real outcomes depend on the sale price, preferences and dilution, and many startups return nothing to common holders.
>
> **Taxes:** incentive stock options (ISOs) and non-qualified options (NSOs) are taxed differently, and early exercise or restricted stock can involve an 83(b) election, which must be filed with the IRS within 30 days of receiving the shares. This is not tax advice; talk to a tax advisor about your situation.

Rules for the writer:
- Never state a future value, a "paper value", or a likely exit price as fact.
- Always show both the option count and the percentage. If the total share count is unknown, show "[ask us for the fully diluted share count]".
- Vesting of 4 years with a 1-year cliff is common, not universal. Use the user's schedule or mark [assumed].

## Negotiation bank

| If they say | You say / do | Lever and limit |
|---|---|---|
| "Can you do better on base?" | "What number would make this a clear yes? And apart from base, is anything else in the way?" Then, if within band: "If I can get to <X>, are you ready to accept?" | Base, up to the band maximum you set. Once. |
| "I have a higher offer from <bigger company>" | "Thanks for telling me. What matters most to you between the two, apart from the number?" Compare on their stated criteria. Match only inside your band. | Don't ask to see the other offer letter as a condition. Don't pretend to match what you can't. |
| "I'd lose my annual bonus / unvested equity if I leave now" | "Let's look at what you'd leave behind. A one-time sign-on can cover that without changing the salary band." | Sign-on bonus, sized to the real loss. Any repayment condition is drafted by counsel; restricted in California since 2026, and other states have their own limits. |
| "Can I get more equity instead?" | Offer a defined trade: "<Y> more options for <Z> less base," both written. | Equity within the pool budget for this level. |
| "I need more time" | "Of course. What do you need to decide, and by when?" Extend once to a specific date for a specific reason. | Honest deadline. |
| "My manager is going to counter" | "What would they have to offer for you to stay? And what made you start looking?" Remind them of their own reasons. | No bidding war. |
| "Can I start in 6 weeks instead of 2?" | Usually yes; ask what would help them start sooner (it's often a vacation they planned; let them take it). | Start date: cheap for you. |
| "What title will I have?" | Use the leveling you decided. If they want "Lead", describe the path and the criteria in writing, not the title. | Title: don't inflate. |
| "Is the equity worth anything?" | Walk through the equity explainer; be honest that many startups return nothing to common holders. | Never a value. |
| "What's the salary range for this role?" | Share it. Many states require it in postings or on request. | Transparency builds trust. |

## Pre-close questions (before the offer call)

1. "Where else are you in process, and what are the timelines?" (Listen for: final rounds this week.)
2. "If you had offers from us and <other>, how would you decide? What are your top 2-3 things?" (Write them down in their words; the offer call answers each.)
3. "What compensation are you looking for?" (Expectation, never history.)
4. "Who else is part of this decision?" (Partner, mentor; offer a call with the team or a founder for them.)
5. "If your current company offered you more to stay, what would you do?"
6. "If we came in around <range>, would that be in the zone, or would something get in the way?"
