# Offer letter checklist

Have counsel review your offer letter template once; then reuse it. General information as of 2026-10, not legal advice. Rules vary by state, and the state where the employee works is usually the one that counts.

## Put in

- Job title, manager (by role or name), full-time status, work location or "remote, based in <state>"
- Start date (proposed; "or another date we agree")
- Base salary as an annual figure, plus pay frequency (e.g. "paid semi-monthly")
- Exempt / non-exempt classification under wage-and-hour law
- Equity: number of options or shares, type if known (ISO / NSO / RSU), vesting schedule and cliff, and the line "subject to approval by the Board and the terms of the company's equity plan and grant agreement". Strike price is set by the board at grant; say so rather than quoting a number you don't control.
- Sign-on bonus: amount and when paid. Any repayment condition is drafted by counsel: restricted in California since 2026 (see below), and other states have their own limits, so counsel checks it
- Benefits in one or two lines with "as described in the plan documents, which may change"
- Contingencies, each stated plainly:
  - satisfactory reference checks
  - background check, only if you run one, with a separate written disclosure and authorization (FCRA) and in line with fair-chance timing rules where they apply
  - proof of identity and authorization to work in the US, completed on Form I-9 within 3 business days of the start date
  - signing the company's confidentiality and invention assignment agreement
- At-will statement where at-will employment applies: either side can end employment at any time, and nothing in the letter changes that
- "This letter and the agreements referenced in it are the complete offer" (integration line, counsel's wording)
- How to accept and the decision date

## Leave out

- Salary history, or any reference to their current pay
- "Guaranteed", "permanent", "long-term", "job security", annual raises or bonuses as certain
- Future equity grants, refreshes, or "we'll revisit your title in 6 months" unless you are ready to commit in writing
- A dollar value for the equity or an expected exit price
- A non-compete for a California worker (unlawful to include since 2024, Business and Professions Code 16600.1); for other states, ask counsel
- Citizenship requirements (ask only about authorization to work, and only through the I-9 process after the offer)
- Anything about health, family, age, or other protected traits

## California hires (verified as of 2026-10)

These apply when the employee primarily lives and works in California, whatever state your company is in. General information, not legal advice.

| Rule | What it means for the offer |
|---|---|
| Labor Code 432.3 | Don't seek salary history, yourself or through an agent. Give the pay scale for the role if the applicant asks. |
| Business and Professions Code 16600.1 and 16600.5 | Non-competes are void, and it is unlawful to include one in an employment contract (limited exceptions). Leave it out of the letter and the template. |
| Labor Code 925 | You can't require a California employee, as a condition of employment, to agree to another state's law or courts for California claims (exception: the employee had their own lawyer negotiate it). Check the governing-law and venue clauses in an out-of-state template. |
| Labor Code 2802 | Reimburse necessary business expenses, including those of working remotely (for example a share of internet and phone). A flat stipend should cover the real cost; counsel confirms the policy. |
| Labor Code 2870 and 2872 | An invention assignment can't reach inventions made entirely on the employee's own time and equipment that don't relate to your business or result from their work. Section 2872 requires giving the employee written notice of this when the agreement is signed. |
| Government Code 12952 (Fair Chance Act) | Employers with 5 or more employees can't ask about conviction history before a conditional offer; after it, an individualized assessment and written notices come before any withdrawal. |
| AB 692 (Labor Code 926; Business and Professions Code 16608), for agreements signed on or after 2026-01-01 | "Stay or pay" repayment terms are mostly banned. A sign-on or retention bonus can be repayable only if: a separate agreement; at least 5 business days' notice of the right to consult a lawyer; prorated; no interest; retention period of 2 years or less; the worker may defer the bonus to the end of the period with no repayment; and repayment only if they quit or are fired for misconduct. |

## Remote and international notes

- A US company hiring a US employee in another state usually needs payroll registration in that state; check before the start date.
- A candidate outside the US is not hired with a US offer letter as is. Local labor law applies. Ask counsel or an employer of record before writing the offer.
