# Classification factors

General information as of 2026-10, not legal advice.

## Sources behind the tests

- IRS common-law rules (behavioral control, financial control, relationship of the parties): https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employee-common-law-employee and Topic 762, https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc762. For a formal IRS determination, a business or worker can file Form SS-8.
- DOL: Field Assistance Bulletin 2025-1 (May 1, 2025) and the proposed rule published February 27, 2026: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/27/2026-03962/employee-or-independent-contractor-status-under-the-fair-labor-standards-act-family-and-medical
- California ABC test: Labor Code 2775 (AB5); willful misclassification penalties: Labor Code 226.8.
- Federal exposure rates: IRC 3509, IRS Internal Revenue Manual 4.23.8: https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-023-008
- VCSP: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/voluntary-classification-settlement-program

## Factor table (score each: Employee-like / Mixed / Contractor-like)

| Group | Factor | Employee-like | Contractor-like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Behavioral control (IRS) / control (DOL, ABC prong A) | Hours and schedule | You set hours or core hours as a requirement | They set their own; you agree on deadlines |
| | Meetings | Required daily standups and team rituals as a condition | Attends meetings needed for the deliverable, by choice |
| | How the work is done | You direct methods, tools, sequence; review like an employee's work | You define the result; they choose how |
| | Training | You train them in your way of working | They bring their own expertise |
| | Evaluation | Performance reviews, goals like staff | Acceptance of deliverables against the statement of work |
| Financial control (IRS) / opportunity for profit or loss (DOL) | Pay structure | Hourly or fixed monthly with no end, like a salary | Fixed price per project or milestone; invoices |
| | Investment and tools | You supply laptop, software licenses, accounts | Own equipment and licenses |
| | Expenses | You reimburse like an employee | They carry their own business expenses |
| | Other clients / market | Works only for you, full time | Current other clients; markets their services |
| | Profit or loss | No way to lose money | Can lose money on a fixed-price job; can earn more by working efficiently |
| | Helpers | Must do the work personally | Can subcontract or hire help |
| Relationship of the parties (IRS) / permanence (DOL) | Term | Indefinite, open-ended | Defined project or term |
| | Benefits | Gets benefits, PTO, equity like staff | None |
| | Integration | Company email, title, on team page or org chart, manages employees | Vendor address, no title, not in the org chart |
| | Core work (ABC prong B; DOL "integral") | Builds the core product you sell | Work outside your main line of business |
| | History | Was your employee doing the same job | Never employed by you |
| Independent business (ABC prong C) | Established trade | No entity, no other clients, no website or rate card | Registered business, other clients, insurance, licenses where relevant |

## How to rate a test (practice rules, not legal thresholds)

- **High**: any of: former employee doing the same work; full-time, indefinite, single-client with set hours; California worker building the core product (prong B).
- **Medium**: control is mixed (some required meetings, your equipment) but they have real independence on money (fixed-price work, other clients).
- **Low**: defined deliverables, own business with other clients, own tools, fixed price, no team integration, and (in ABC states) work outside your usual course of business.

Don't net factors against each other like votes. One heavy fact (prong B in California, former employee) outweighs several light ones.

## Questions to ask the user if missing (pick at most 3)
1. Where does this person live and work (state or country)?
2. Do they have other active clients, and their own registered business?
3. Is there a signed agreement that assigns the code and IP to the company?

## State tests for common states (as of 2026-10)

General information, not legal advice. Each state can use different tests for different laws (unemployment insurance, wage payment, workers' compensation); this table names the main one and its source. For any state not listed, say "check your state's test" and add it to the questions for counsel.

| State | Main test | Source |
|---|---|---|
| California | ABC test: presumed employee unless free from control (A), work outside the usual course of the hirer's business (B), and customarily engaged in an independent trade (C) | Labor Code 2775 (AB5) |
| New Jersey | ABC test for unemployment insurance, also applied to the state wage laws; prong B can also be met if the work is done outside all of the hirer's places of business | N.J.S.A. 43:21-19(i)(6); Hargrove v. Sleepy's (N.J. 2015) |
| Massachusetts | ABC-style three-prong test; all three prongs must be met, and the employer carries the burden | M.G.L. c. 149, s. 148B |
| Illinois | Three-prong ABC-style test for wage payment and for unemployment insurance (prong B can also be met by work outside all of the hirer's places of business) | 820 ILCS 115/2; 820 ILCS 405/212 |
| New York | Common-law test: does the hirer have the right to supervise, direct and control the work (the Department of Labor looks at all facts, and a contract label does not decide it) | NY Department of Labor, unemployment insurance guidance (dol.ny.gov) |
| Washington | Unemployment insurance, two alternative routes. Route 1: free from control, AND outside the usual course of business or outside all places of business, AND an independently established trade of the same nature. Route 2: free from control, AND (outside the usual course, or outside all places of business, or the worker pays for their own principal place of business), AND (an independent trade, or a principal place of business eligible for a federal business deduction), AND an IRS business-expense schedule, AND a state Department of Revenue account with a UBI number, AND separate books for the business. All conditions of a route must hold. | RCW 50.04.140 (1) and (2) |
| Oregon | For revenue, unemployment and some licensing laws: free from direction and control over means and manner, an "independently established business" (at least 3 of 5 listed criteria: separate business location, financial risk, multiple clients or marketing, substantial investment, authority to hire), and required licenses. For wage claims, the state labor bureau (BOLI) uses the federal economic-reality test | ORS 670.600; Oregon BOLI guidance |
| Texas | Unemployment insurance: employment unless the worker is shown to be free from direction or control, under the contract and in fact; the Texas Workforce Commission applies a twenty-factor control test | Texas Labor Code 201.041; Texas Workforce Commission guidance |
| Florida | Reemployment (unemployment) tax: common-law right-of-control test; control over how the work is done is the most important factor | Chapter 443, Florida Statutes; Florida Department of Revenue guidance |

Rule of thumb for engineers: in ABC states (CA, NJ, MA, IL for wage claims), a developer building the hirer's core product rarely meets prong B unless the "outside all places of business" alternative exists and truly applies (NJ, IL). Do not assume a remote worker's home counts as "outside the places of business"; ask counsel.
