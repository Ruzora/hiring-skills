**State test (California ABC, Labor Code 2775, as of 2026-10):** California presumes a worker is an employee unless all three prongs below hold.
- **A (free from control):** Likely fails. You set his tickets, review his work and require standups.
- **B (outside the usual course of your business):** Very hard to meet. He writes the core product of a software company.
- **C (independent trade):** Likely fails. He has no entity and no known other clients.

AB5 has exemptions, such as business-to-business, with strict conditions. With no entity and no other clients, those look hard to meet, but whether any exemption applies is a question for counsel.

Willful misclassification in California can add civil penalties of $5,000 to $15,000 per violation, or $10,000 to $25,000 for a pattern or practice (Labor Code 226.8).

**Facts that would change this:**
- If he has real, current other clients and his own business, prong C and the federal tests would improve. Prong B would still fail, so the California rating stays High.
- If he sets his own hours and standups are optional, prong A would be weaker, but the California rating would still be High.
- Unknown expenses, benefits and helper rights are unlikely to move any rating.
