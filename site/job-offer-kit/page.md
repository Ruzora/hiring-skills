# Job offer email template and offer kit for engineering hires

Job Offer Kit is a free AI skill that writes a job offer email for an engineering hire, plus everything around it: pre-close questions, an offer call script, equity explained in plain words with your numbers, and a negotiation playbook. It also fixes the common mistakes first, like asking for current salary or a 24-hour exploding deadline.

An offer email on its own is the easy part. Engineers you want usually decide on the call before it and the conversations after it.

## What you get

Tell the skill the role, your salary band, the equity grant and what you know about the candidate. In about 10 minutes you get:

- **Changes to your plan:** anything risky, fixed, with a one-line reason
- **Pre-close questions** for a 10-minute call before the offer
- **Your numbers sheet** (internal): opening offer, flex order, walk-away, honest deadline
- **A verbal offer script** and a **paste-ready offer email**
- **An offer letter checklist:** what goes in, what stays out
- **Equity in plain words** with your actual numbers
- **A negotiation playbook:** if they say X, you say Y, with the lever and its limit
- **A close plan** from verbal yes to day one

## How it works

**Pre-close before you offer.** Three things must be known before you name a number: what else is in play and when, how the candidate will decide (in their words), and what they'd do if their employer counters. An offer made without them is a guess. Then move fast: verbal offer within one or two working days of the final interview, written offer the same day.

**Never ask for salary history.** Many states and cities ban it, and for a remote hire the candidate's location usually counts. Price from your band.

**Trade, don't concede.** "If I can get to X, are you ready to accept?" Each lever has a job. A sign-on bonus covers a one-time loss, like a bonus they forfeit by leaving, without breaking your salary band; in California, making it repayable has been tightly restricted since 2026 (AB 692). Start date is cheap for you. Title is the expensive one.

**Honest deadlines.** About 3 to 5 business days, with the real reason, extended once for a concrete reason. A 24-hour exploding offer tells a senior engineer how they'll be treated.

**Counteroffers raised early.** Ask about them in the pre-close and have the candidate rehearse the resignation. If a counter arrives, go back to their own reasons for looking.

**Equity in both units.** Option count and percentage of fully diluted shares, plus strike, cliff, exercise window and dilution. Never what the equity "will be worth". If early exercise applies, it notes that an 83(b) election must be filed with the IRS within 30 days. Not tax advice.

**Paper that matches the promise.** Nothing important stays verbal. The letter avoids "guaranteed", lists contingencies (including the Form I-9 check within 3 business days of the start date) and never conditions the offer on citizenship. For California employees, a non-compete is unlawful to include since 2024. General information as of 2026-10, not legal or tax advice.

**How this compares.** A job offer email template gives you a polite paragraph with blanks. A chatbot asked cold usually writes exactly what you asked for, salary-history question and 24-hour deadline included. This skill checks the plan first.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should a job offer email include?**
Title, start date, base salary and pay frequency, the equity grant with vesting, benefits, contingencies, the decision date and how to accept, with the formal letter attached.

**How long should a candidate have to accept a job offer?**
Three to five business days is a fair default for a senior engineer. Give the real reason for the date and extend once if they ask for a specific reason.

**Can I ask a candidate what they earn now?**
In many states and cities, no. Ask what they're looking for instead, and price from your own band.
