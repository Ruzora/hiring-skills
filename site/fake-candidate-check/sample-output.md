**Dropped from the log (not a signal):**
- Accent you could not place: an accent is never evidence and using it is national-origin discrimination.
- Slack English more polished than spoken English: many people write better than they speak in a second language, and many use grammar tools.
- Blurred background on Sep 18: you had not asked every candidate to turn it off yet; many real candidates use one.
- A few seconds of lag before answers: a slow connection or pauses on their own are not a signal.
- Your cofounder's view that the accent and polished Slack English mean he is "probably not in the US": that rests only on not-a-signal items, so it cannot be the reason to drop him. The real concerns are the shared phone number, the change in appearance, the laptop address and the payment request, and each one has a fair check that can clear him or confirm a problem.

## If a check fails
- What to say: "Thanks for your patience. We weren't able to complete this step today. Could we try another option, such as a call with a second person from our team, meeting in person, or an identity-verification service?"
- What to do next: offer the alternatives in that order (in person, verified video with a second interviewer, notary or identity-verification service). Treat a broken webcam, expired ID or new number as a reason to retry, not an accusation. If every alternative is refused without a reason, or the checks show a mismatch, the decision is yours and your cofounder's; give no access in the meantime.
- Report: if you suspect a North Korean IT worker scheme or stolen identity, report it at ic3.gov.
